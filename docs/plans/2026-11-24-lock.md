### Body → sky
`body-sky.js` (`BodySky.bodySky(body, hour)`): readiness → low cloud regime (≥ 80 fair, 65–79 partly, 50–64 stratocumulus, < 50 stratus); sleep → morning valley fog (from sleep score 78 down); HRV → cirrus form; strain/steps → cumulus growth through the day (pace 1.15); skin temp + resting HR → haze (+0.3 °C, +3), light rain for likely illness (+0.5 °C, +5), the storm under readiness 35 (D2 as amended in ST1); new formation per day from the date; no readiness → a plain fair sky; no usual resting HR → no haze/rain/storm (FW1). Tap the sky to explain (D4).

- First week minimums (FW1): readiness from day 4, normals from day 8, usual bedtime from day 5, readiness's own normal from day 11.

## 3. The renderer path (decided: A)

The open question from CLAUDE.md was AGSL (API 33, no 3D textures) or baked frames. Compared:

| | **A · GLES 3.0 port (picked)** | B · AGSL | C · Baked on the PC, shipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaders | **Reused as written**: WebGL2 is GLSL ES 3.00, the same language as GLES 3.0 | Rewrite in AGSL (no 3D textures → 2D atlas at reduced resolution, no MRT, no float accumulation) | None on the phone beyond the cheap passes |
| API reach | 26+ (GLES 3.0 is on every target device); half-float targets need `EXT_color_buffer_half_float` (check at start, fall back to bakes) | 33+ only; 26–32 still need a GLES path | 26+ |
| Live sky | Yes (as on the PC) | Reduced | No: sky fixed to the shipped set |
| Bake on device | Yes (3.13: ~16 s GPU per keyframe on the S21) | No | Not needed |
| Size | Code + noise textures (generated) | Code | A fixed library: e.g. 7 formations × 4 weathers × ~20 MB ≈ 560 MB |
| Risk | Port effort (1.5k lines of JS to Kotlin), device compile time, heat | Rewrite and quality loss | APK size; skies repeat, D3 lost |

**A:** one renderer for every API level, the shaders as they are, the bake/live split the prototype proved on the S21 (3.12–3.13: baked page 59–61 fps, post 7 ms). Live render when the day's keyframes are not baked, bakes otherwise.

### The glass (decided: drawn from the renderer's own blur)
On Android a Compose layer cannot blur what a GL surface drew under it (no backdrop filter; `RenderEffect` blurs a view's own content, API 31+). Haze (well maintained, 2.1.0 on 2026-11-02) falls back to a tint on Android 11 and below by default, its opt-in older blur is not real-time, it would blur the whole screen a second time on top of the sky (more heat), and nothing shows it can see a GL surface's pixels. So, best for the user:
- The renderer draws offscreen into two GPU images per frame (`ImageReader` with GPU usage, `HardwareBuffer`): the scene, and the scene blurred 22 px with the hour's saturation (from its existing blur pyramid, at low resolution).
- Compose draws the scene image as the background (`Bitmap.wrapHardwareBuffer`, API 29), and each glass card draws its own window of the blurred image, clipped to its shape, then the hour's tint, light edge and shadow, then its text. Sky and glass come from the same frame and are drawn in the same Compose pass: the glass cannot lag the scroll.
- The renderer reports the page L\* under the cards (a downsampled read, about once a second) for `GlassRule`.
- **Android 8–9 (API 26–28):** no `wrapHardwareBuffer`. The scene shows in a `TextureView`; cards get the hour's tint without blur, at a strength measured to keep 3.0:1 (decided 2026-11-24).
- **Fallback if the spike fails:** Haze over the scene image (needs no further approval: the user allows a well-maintained dependency).

### Bakes (decided: the likely sky only)
- One weather's set as now baked: ~20 MB (fair, 16 keyframes); cloudier skies up to ~2 MB per keyframe (~30 MB). All four weathers: ~80–130 MB. The likely one saves ~75%, over the user's 50% bar, so: **the likely sky only**, one day kept.
- A bake is the whole sky, not only the weather type: sleep sets the fog, HRV the cirrus, skin temperature and resting HR the haze, all known only after the morning sync. In the sample data, tomorrow's whole morning sky equals today's on **45 of 59 days (76%)**; the weather type alone on 48 of 59 (81%).
- So: at night on the charger (WorkManager: charging + idle) bake tomorrow's likely sky (today's body state, tomorrow's formation seed). After the morning sync, if the state differs (~1 morning in 4), render live at once (~9 s of full GPU on the S21 to converge) and bake the right set in the background.
- Afternoons ahead of your usual pace (14 of 58 in the sample) build cumulus: those hours render live (or re-bake when it triggers).

## 4. Order of work after "lock"

Ships **screen by screen behind a "Sky (preview)" switch** in Settings (decided 2026-11-24): with the switch off the app is the current one; each new screen appears with the switch on as it lands; the old screens are deleted at the end.

1. **Domain ports + parity tests** (ship at once, under the current look): S2, normals band, BodySky, CoachRules, GlassRule. The S2 sleep score then shows in the current screens, so its 7-night review (TODOS.md) starts.
2. **Spikes on the S21:** renderer A (shader compile time, the 3D texture build on the device, half-float support, frame cost at 412×915 ×2); the two-image hand-off to Compose and one glass card over the live scene while scrolling (frame cost, the hand-off's delay).
3. **Renderer port** behind the switch: live scene, then bakes and the bake worker, then tilt, transitions, reduced motion.
4. **Design system**: `DESIGN.md` rewrite (Sky Glass), tokens, the 12 sp floor, glass components; the switch in Settings.
5. **Screens** in the order of §2, each its own PR with device shots at the four hours.
6. **Phone verification** for each screen (§5); then remove the switch and the old screens.

## 5. How it is checked
- Unit: parity fixtures (§2); the existing domain tests stay green.
- Contrast on the device: a debug build measures text over the rendered frame the way `glass-check.js` does (worst pixel under each text box, against the glass formula) and fails under 3.0. Run at 07:15, 15:10, 19:45, 20:00, 20:30, 23:10, 05:20, 05:45 on the six drawn days; also the API 26–28 tint.
- Frame cost and heat on the S21 for every renderer change (GPU ms per frame, skin temperature over 3 minutes, as 3.12).
- Device shots per screen and hour against the boards.

## 6. Decisions (2026-11-24)
1. **Renderer: A**, the GLES 3.0 port (shaders unchanged, API 26+, live + bakes made on the phone).
2. **Glass: drawn from the renderer's own blur**, handed to Compose as GPU images (best for the user: no lag, blur on every Android 10+, the measured look, less heat). Haze is the fallback; the user accepts a well-maintained dependency.
3. **Bakes: the likely sky only** (saves ~75%, over the 50% bar), baked at night, corrected after the morning sync.
4. **Shipping: screen by screen** behind a "Sky (preview)" switch; logic and S2 first.
5. **Android 8–9:** the hour's tint without blur.

## 7. Risks
- **Device shader compile:** T9 cut headless compile to ~25 s on the PC; the S21 was never measured (open since T9). If it is long, compile once and cache program binaries (`glProgramBinary`), and show the last bake meanwhile.
- **Heat and battery:** live rendering warms the S21 by ~5 °C in minutes (3.13); play bakes whenever possible and stop the live render when hidden. The morning miss (~1 in 4) costs one live render plus a background bake.
- **The image hand-off:** one frame of delay between the GL frame and Compose is fine (scene and glass stay together); the spike measures its cost.
- **Mixed look during the switch period:** expected; only with the switch on.
- **S2 on real nights:** tuned on prototype nights; review after 7 real nights (TODOS.md).
