Round G3 · The sky behind the text after sunset

Why the veil was there. The number and header sit on the hero cloud. After sunset it stayed brighter than any ink allows (light ink needs the sky under it at L* 59 or less for 3:1). Four things kept it bright, all in the scene: the clouds' sky light mixed 55% white at every hour; the set sun's light faded linearly from dusk to night, so cloud tops stayed sunlit next to sky gaps that were already dark; stars were full strength (L* 70–78 points under the text); and falling snow stayed pure white at night (L* 96 specks on an L* 41 sky).

G3, in the scene, not on the UI. After sunset the sky light on cloud tops is the dark sky, not white. Once the sun is down, its light leaves the clouds before the sky goes dark, and the moon takes over at full night. Stars are low intensity, as in the reference. Night snow is lit like the moonlit cloud (L* ~60). Day is unchanged (mean pixel difference ≈ 1 level).

Result at the 3.0 floor: no veil from 20:15 through the night to 06:15, in clear, partly cloudy, rain and snow. A thin veil (5–14%) is left only around 20:00, the minute the ink swaps from dark to light.
